Background: Maurice Gontran was an only son who died at the age of eighteen from tuberculosis. He was exceptionally intelligent, mature beyond his years, gentle, affectionate, and deeply devoted to study. He had successfully completed his early education and was preparing for admission to the prestigious Polytechnic School when illness ended his life on Earth.

Background: His parents were devastated by his death. Because Maurice had always been physically delicate, they blamed themselves for encouraging his intense studies, believing that overwork had caused his premature death. They bitterly regretted his education and wondered what use all his learning had been now that he was gone. Later, however, after discovering Spiritism, they found comfort through the communications received from their son.

Allan: My dear Maurice, I know how deeply attached you were to your parents.

Allan: I can't doubt that you would wish to comfort them if possible.

Allan: Their grief — almost despair — after your death has damaged their health and made them lose interest in life itself.

Allan: Perhaps a few loving words from you could restore hope to them.

Maurice Gontran: Dear old friend...

Maurice Gontran: I have been waiting impatiently for this opportunity to communicate with them.

Maurice Gontran: My parents’ sorrow deeply pains me.

Maurice Gontran: But their grief will soften once they truly understand that I'm not lost to them.

Maurice Gontran: You must help convince them of this truth.

Maurice Gontran: And I already foresee that you will succeed.

Maurice Gontran: This painful event was necessary in order to lead them toward a belief that will eventually bring them peace and happiness.

Maurice Gontran: It was meant to prevent them from rebelling against the wisdom of God.

Maurice Gontran: As you know, my father was very skeptical about life after death.

Maurice Gontran: God allowed this sorrow to reach him in order to awaken him from that error.

Maurice Gontran: We will meet again in the world where human suffering no longer exists.

Maurice Gontran: I have merely gone there before them.

Maurice Gontran: But tell them this clearly:

Maurice Gontran: If they continue doubting the goodness of God, they will delay the joy of seeing me again.

Maurice Gontran: Such distrust could even prevent me from communicating with them during the remainder of their lives on Earth.

Maurice Gontran: Despair is a rebellion against the will of God.

Maurice Gontran: And such rebellion always prolongs the suffering that caused the despair until the soul finally accepts the trial imposed upon it.

Maurice Gontran: Despair is a kind of suicide.

Maurice Gontran: It destroys the health of the body.

Maurice Gontran: And the person who shortens his life hoping to escape sorrow more quickly prepares only a terrible disappointment for himself.

Maurice Gontran: On the contrary, one must preserve physical strength in order to better endure earthly trials.

Maurice Gontran: My beloved parents...

Maurice Gontran: It is now directly to you that I speak.

Maurice Gontran: From the moment I left my physical body, I have never ceased being near you.

Maurice Gontran: In fact, I'm with you even more often now than when I lived on Earth.

Maurice Gontran: So, be comforted.

Maurice Gontran: I'm not dead.

Maurice Gontran: I'm more alive than you are.

Maurice Gontran: Only my body has died.

Maurice Gontran: My soul continues to live.

Maurice Gontran: I'm free.

Maurice Gontran: I'm happy.

Maurice Gontran: I'm beyond the reach of disease, weakness, and pain.

Maurice Gontran: Instead of grieving my departure, you should rejoice knowing that I now live in a place free from anxiety and suffering...

Maurice Gontran: A place where the heart overflows with pure and complete happiness.

Maurice Gontran: Ah, my friends...

Maurice Gontran: Don't mourn excessively for those who die young.

Maurice Gontran: It is often a mercy granted by God to souls who no longer need the trials of life on Earth.

Maurice Gontran: My final life on Earth was not meant to continue any longer.

Maurice Gontran: I had already acquired everything necessary to prepare me for a more important mission that I will fulfill later.

Maurice Gontran: Had I remained longer on Earth, who knows what dangerous temptations I might have encountered?

Maurice Gontran: Perhaps I was not yet strong enough to resist all the seductions of life on Earth.

Maurice Gontran: I might have fallen...

Maurice Gontran: And delayed my spiritual progress for hundreds of years.

Maurice Gontran: Why should those who love me regret what has become such a blessing for me?

Maurice Gontran: Endless grief in such circumstances reflects a lack of faith.

Maurice Gontran: Only those who believe in total annihilation after death are truly without consolation.

Maurice Gontran: Such people deserve great pity...

Maurice Gontran: Because for them, those they love are lost forever.

Maurice Gontran: The grave destroys their final hope.

Maurice Gontran: No.

Maurice Gontran: I suffered only from the illness before dying.

Maurice Gontran: But as the final moments approached, even that suffering gradually weakened.

Maurice Gontran: Then one day, I simply fell asleep without thinking about death.

Maurice Gontran: I began to dream.

Maurice Gontran: His face was no longer thin and worn from age.

Maurice Gontran: He opened his arms and embraced me joyfully.

Maurice Gontran: Many other people came with him.

Maurice Gontran: All of them smiled warmly as they welcomed me.

Maurice Gontran: I seemed to recognize them.

Maurice Gontran: I felt happy to see them again.

Maurice Gontran: What I believed to be a dream was actually reality.

Maurice Gontran: I was never again to awaken on Earth.

Maurice Gontran: Instead, I had awakened within the spirit world.

Allan: Was your illness caused by excessive study?

Maurice Gontran: No.

Maurice Gontran: Be completely certain that it was not.

Maurice Gontran: The length of my life on Earth had already been determined beforehand.

Maurice Gontran: Nothing could have kept me on Earth any longer.

Maurice Gontran: During the moments when my spirit partially detached itself during bodily sleep, I already knew this inwardly.

Maurice Gontran: And my spirit rejoiced at the thought of its approaching liberation.

Maurice Gontran: But my time on Earth was not wasted.

Maurice Gontran: I'm now very grateful that I did not neglect my studies.

Maurice Gontran: The knowledge I acquired strengthened my soul and expanded my understanding.

Maurice Gontran: That knowledge was not lost.

Maurice Gontran: Even though I could not fully use it during my short life on Earth, I will apply it even more effectively during a future existence.

Maurice Gontran: Farewell, dear friend.

Maurice Gontran: I'm now going to visit my parents in order to prepare them to receive this communication.
