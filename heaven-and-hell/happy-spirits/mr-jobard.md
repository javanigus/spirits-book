Brussels, 1861. Mr. Jobard, director of the Industrial Museum of Brussels and honorary president of the Spiritist Society of Paris, died suddenly from apoplexy at the age of sixty-nine. Shortly after his death, his spirit communicated spontaneously through a medium before the Society even had the chance to evoke him.

Mr. Jobard: I'm here... the very one you were about to evoke.

Mr. Jobard: I especially wished to communicate through this medium, although until now I had tried in vain to make him write for me.

Mr. Jobard: I want to describe what I felt at the moment my soul separated from my body.

Mr. Jobard: I felt an impossible-to-describe trembling throughout my entire being.

Mr. Jobard: My whole life... my birth, youth, and adult years... suddenly returned to my memory all at once.

Mr. Jobard: Every event of my life appeared before me with extraordinary clarity.

Mr. Jobard: I desired only one thing... to find myself again in the regions revealed to us through our beloved Spiritist belief.

Mr. Jobard: Then all the agitation faded away.

Mr. Jobard: I was free.

Mr. Jobard: And my lifeless body lay beside me.

Mr. Jobard: Ah, dear friends... what overwhelming happiness it is to cast off the burden of the earthly body!

Mr. Jobard: What impossible-to-describe joy it is to contemplate the glorious vastness surrounding us!

Mr. Jobard: But don't imagine that I immediately reached the highest state of happiness.

Mr. Jobard: No.

Mr. Jobard: I'm among those who have learned something... but who still have much more to learn.

Mr. Jobard: I quickly remembered you, my brothers in exile on Earth.

Mr. Jobard: My sympathy and good wishes remain with you.

After Mr. Jobard’s funeral ceremony, several members of the Society gathered together and unexpectedly received a spontaneous communication from another spirit none of them had ever heard of before.

Bernard: My name is Bernard.

Bernard: I lived in Passy in the year 1796, when it was still a small village.

Bernard: I was a poor schoolteacher.

Bernard: God alone knows how much misery I endured.

Bernard: What a weary life I lived.

Bernard: How many years of anxiety and suffering I passed through.

Bernard: Eventually, I began cursing God, the devil, mankind in general... and women in particular.

Bernard: Not one person ever came to me and said, “Courage... patience.”

Bernard: I was forced to live alone... always alone.

Bernard: And the cruelty of others eventually made me cruel as well.

Bernard: Since then, I wander through the places where I once lived and died.

Bernard: Today I heard everything you said.

Bernard: Your prayers deeply moved me.

Bernard: You accompanied a good and worthy spirit to the grave of his body.

Bernard: Everything you said and did touched me greatly.

Bernard: I was among a large group of spirits.

Bernard: Together, we all prayed for you... and for the spread of your holy belief.

Bernard: Pray for us... for we need help.

Bernard: The spirit of Sanson, who was with us, promised that you would think of us.

Bernard: I wish to reincarnate so that I may make better use of a new earthly trial.

Bernard: In this way, I hope to prepare a happier future for myself in the spirit world.

Bernard: Farewell, friends.

Bernard: I call you friends because you are kind to those who suffer.

Bernard: I wish you good thoughts... and a happy future.

Allan Kardec later explained that the writing medium had never met Mr. Sanson and knew nothing about his life, family, or beliefs. The medium had also never heard of the spirit Bernard. Kardec presented these details as evidence that the communications could not have come from the medium’s imagination.

Later, Mr. Jobard was formally evoked and questioned by the Society.

Allan: During your life on Earth, you asked us to evoke you after your death.

Allan: We gladly do so, not only to honor your request, but also because we sincerely care for you.

Allan: We also hope to learn from you, because you are especially capable of giving us precise information about the spirit world.

Allan: We'd be grateful if you would answer our questions.

Mr. Jobard: What matters now is helping you better understand spirit life.

Mr. Jobard: As for your sympathy... I see it clearly.

Mr. Jobard: I no longer receive it merely through the ears.

Mr. Jobard: That alone is already a great advancement.

Allan: To help us understand clearly, can you tell us where you are in this room?

Allan: And if we could see you, how would you appear?

Mr. Jobard: I'm close beside the medium.

Mr. Jobard: You would see me in the appearance of the Jobard who so often sat at this table.

Mr. Jobard: Your physical eyes, not yet opened to spirit sight, can perceive spirits only under their earthly form.

Allan: Would it be possible for you to make yourself visible to us?

Allan: And if not, what prevents it?

Mr. Jobard: Your own condition prevents it.

Mr. Jobard: A spiritually aware medium could see me.

Mr. Jobard: Others could not.

Allan: The seat you now occupy is the same seat you occupied while alive.

Allan: We intentionally reserved it for you this evening.

Allan: Those who once saw you there may imagine you beside us now exactly as before.

Allan: Though your physical body is gone, your fluidic body still has the same form.

Allan: Even though we can't see you with our physical eyes, we perceive you with the eyes of thought.

Allan: And although we can no longer speak with you directly, we can still communicate through writing with the help of a medium.

Allan: So, our connection with you has not truly been interrupted by death.

Allan: We can converse with you almost as easily and completely as before.

Allan: Is this understanding correct?

Mr. Jobard: Yes.

Mr. Jobard: You have understood this for a long time already.

Mr. Jobard: And as for this seat... I will often occupy it, even when you don't notice me there.

Mr. Jobard: My spirit will remain among you.

Kardec explains that this statement should not be understood figuratively. Spiritism teaches that spirits may literally remain present among the living through their fluidic or spiritual body. Some spirits stay near people they love, influencing, protecting, and inspiring them.

Allan: It has not been long since you last sat here among us while alive.

Allan: Do your present surroundings feel very different from your former earthly state?

Allan: What effect does this change have on you?

Mr. Jobard: The surroundings themselves don't seem all that different to me.

Mr. Jobard: But my spirit now possesses a clearness and sharpness of perception that leaves no uncertainty in whatever I examine with thought.

Allan: Do you remember having existed in the same state before your last life on Earth?

Allan: And do you feel changed since then?

Mr. Jobard: I remember my previous existences.

Mr. Jobard: And I can see that I have improved.

Mr. Jobard: I now see clearly... and I absorb what I perceive.

Mr. Jobard: After my earlier incarnations, my spirit remained in confusion.

Mr. Jobard: I saw nothing except the gaps and failures of my life on Earth.

Allan: Do you remember the incarnation that came before your life as Mr. Jobard?

Mr. Jobard: In that previous existence, I was a mechanic laborer consumed by poverty and by the desire to perfect my work.

Mr. Jobard: During my life as Jobard, I fulfilled the dreams of that poor workman.

Mr. Jobard: And I praise God, whose infinite goodness allowed the seed planted in my mind to grow and bear fruit.

Allan: Have you already given communications elsewhere?

Mr. Jobard: Very few so far.

Mr. Jobard: In many places, another spirit has taken my name.

Mr. Jobard: In some cases, I was nearby but unable to communicate.

Mr. Jobard: My death is still recent, and I remain affected by certain earthly influences.

Mr. Jobard: I can express my thoughts only where I find complete sympathy.

Mr. Jobard: Soon I will act with greater freedom... but not yet.

Mr. Jobard: When a well-known man dies, he is evoked everywhere.

Mr. Jobard: Thousands of spirits are eager to assume his identity.

Mr. Jobard: This has already happened in my case several times.

Mr. Jobard: I assure you that very few spirits are able to communicate directly immediately after death, even through their preferred medium.

Allan: Do you see the spirits who are with us this evening?

Mr. Jobard: Yes.

Mr. Jobard: Near you I see Lazarus and Erastes.

Mr. Jobard: A little farther away, hovering in space, I see the Spirit of Truth.

Mr. Jobard: Besides these more advanced spirits, I also see many spirit-friends surrounding this gathering.

Mr. Jobard: They participate actively and kindly in your work.

Mr. Jobard: You should consider yourselves fortunate, dear friends.

Mr. Jobard: Good influences surround you and protect you from suggestions that might lead you into error.

Allan: During your life, you believed the Earth was formed through the merging of four planets.

Allan: Do you still hold this opinion?

Mr. Jobard: No.

Mr. Jobard: That opinion was mistaken.

Mr. Jobard: Recent geological discoveries already prove the upheavals and successive eras through which the Earth developed.

Mr. Jobard: The Earth, like all planets, has had its own independent life.

Mr. Jobard: God had no need of such a chaotic catastrophe as the collision of several planets.

Mr. Jobard: Water and fire alone are the organic elements of the Earth.

Allan: You also believed that human beings could remain indefinitely in a cataleptic state.

Allan: And you believed humanity had been brought to Earth while in that condition.

Allan: Do you still believe this?

Mr. Jobard: No.

Mr. Jobard: Those ideas were illusions of my imagination, which was always inclined toward exaggeration.

Mr. Jobard: Catalepsy can last a long time... but not forever.

Mr. Jobard: My theory came from exaggerated Eastern legends.

Mr. Jobard: Believe me, I have suffered greatly in realizing how quickly I accepted such illusions.

Mr. Jobard: Don't forget this lesson.

Mr. Jobard: I possessed considerable knowledge.

Mr. Jobard: And my intelligence, without vanity, was quick and active because of the broad studies of my previous lives.

Mr. Jobard: But from earlier incarnations I had also retained a fascination for the marvelous, the complicated, and the fantastic creations of human imagination.

Mr. Jobard: As yet, I have not devoted much attention to purely intellectual matters such as those that interest you.

Mr. Jobard: How could I, when I'm dazzled and carried away by the wonderful spectacle surrounding me?

Mr. Jobard: The bond of our shared Spiritist belief... a bond far stronger than human beings can imagine... is the only thing capable of drawing me back toward this Earth that I'm now leaving behind.

Mr. Jobard: And I leave it not with joy, for that would show irreverence toward the Creator...

Mr. Jobard: But with deep gratitude for my freedom.

In February 1862, the Spiritist Society organized a collection to help struggling workers in Lyons. One member donated twenty-five francs in Mr. Jobard’s name, after which Jobard dictated another message.

Mr. Jobard: I'm pleased and grateful that my Spiritist brothers have not forgotten me.

Mr. Jobard: I thank the generous heart that conveyed the offering I myself would have made had I still lived among you.

Mr. Jobard: In the world where I now live, we have no need for money.

Mr. Jobard: So I had to borrow from the purse of a friend in order to give you visible proof of my sympathy for my brothers in Lyons.

Mr. Jobard: Brave workers... you now see that charity is not an empty word.

Mr. Jobard: Rich and poor alike have shown you brotherly sympathy during your suffering.

Mr. Jobard: You are therefore walking upon the great humanitarian road of progress.

Mr. Jobard: May God preserve you upon that path.

Mr. Jobard: And may happier times await you.

Mr. Jobard: Our spirit friends will support and assist you in overcoming the hardships of your condition.

Mr. Jobard: I now live more peacefully.

Mr. Jobard: I'm less disturbed by the constant evocations that pursued me from every direction like a storm of hail.

Mr. Jobard: Apparently, spirits are not exempt from the tyranny of fashion.

Mr. Jobard: When the fashion of evoking Jobard is replaced by someone else, I will pass into human forgetfulness.

Mr. Jobard: And when that day comes, I ask my sincere and serious friends to continue evoking me.

Mr. Jobard: Then we may resume our study of questions that until now have been treated too superficially.

Mr. Jobard: And your friend Jobard, now completely transformed, may continue being useful to you from the depths of his heart.
