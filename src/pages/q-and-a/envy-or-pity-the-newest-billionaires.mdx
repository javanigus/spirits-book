# Anthropic's Founders Are Becoming Billionaires. Should We Envy Them—or Pity Them?

*According to The Spirits' Book and Heaven and Hell*

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-intro-01.webp" alt="Envy or pity? Two smiling people in front of a glass office building labeled AI Anthropic, with a rising IPO chart, a yacht, a mansion and stacks of money" maxWidth={1100} />

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind Claude, has just made history with the largest IPO ever recorded, creating extraordinary fortunes for its founders and early investors. Most people would envy anyone who suddenly becomes a billionaire. But what if enormous wealth isn't necessarily a blessing? What if it's actually one of the most dangerous tests a person can face? And what if, instead of envying the ultra-rich, we should actually pity them?

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-intro-02.webp" alt="The Spirits' Book, Heaven and Hell and The Mediums' Book, each compiled by Allan Kardec, beside the text: The perspectives presented are based on three books compiled by Allan Kardec. To learn more, visit lifedeathbeyondbook.com." maxWidth={1100} />

The perspectives presented are based on three books compiled by Allan Kardec: The Spirits' Book, Heaven and Hell, and The Mediums' Book. These works contain teachings attributed to highly advanced spirits about God, life, death, reincarnation, and the purpose of our existence. To learn more, visit Life Death Beyond Book dot com.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-01.webp" alt="Envy… or pity? A gold mansion and a private jet under a night sky" maxWidth={1100} />

Why would a person with billions of dollars, almost unlimited choices, and the ability to live in extraordinary comfort deserve pity?

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-02.webp" alt="“We don't know what awaits them.” A glowing figure on a gold pedestal, its long shadow behind a curtain, with a crowd looking up" maxWidth={1100} />

In The Spirits' Book, the spirits discuss people we envy because they appear to be fortunate. The answer challenges that envy directly. We don't know what awaits them, the spirits explain. If they use their wealth only for themselves, their apparent success may conceal a serious spiritual failure.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-03.webp" alt="Statement card: Pity, rather than envy — for prosperity that serves only selfish desires" maxWidth={1100} />

The book even tells readers to pity rather than envy people whose prosperity serves only their own selfish desires.

## Two kinds of trials

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-04.webp" alt="Part 1, Two kinds of trials. Wealth and poverty are both trials. A balance scale with bread on one side and gold coins on the other" maxWidth={1100} />

That warning is not based on the idea that money is inherently bad. According to the spirits, wealth and poverty are both trials, and both bring moral dangers. Poverty can provoke bitterness and resentment. Wealth can encourage pride, extravagance, and the feeling that no amount is ever enough.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-05.webp" alt="Part 1, Two kinds of trials. More power to help others. Cupped hands holding coins, linked to people around them" maxWidth={1100} />

And wealth presents a particular challenge: a wealthy person has opportunities to help others that a poor person simply does not have.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-06.webp" alt="Part 1, Two kinds of trials. Desires grow with the fortune. A figure climbing rising coin stacks, thinking “more”" maxWidth={1100} />

The spirits describe people whose desires grow along with their fortunes. The more they acquire, the more they want. Instead of feeling satisfied, they can become increasingly consumed by accumulation.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-07.webp" alt="Part 1, Two kinds of trials. The real issue is responsibility. A glowing balance scale in front of faded coin stacks" maxWidth={1100} />

But the deeper issue is not how much money a person has. It is the responsibility that comes with having it.

## A deposit to account for

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-08.webp" alt="Part 2, A deposit to account for. Wealth is a deposit. Cupped hands of coins under a beam of light, tagged “On loan”" maxWidth={1100} />

In The Spirits' Book, the spirits explain that wealth is a deposit for which its holder must render an account. The owner is responsible not only for using money wisely, but also for the good that could have been accomplished with it.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-09.webp" alt="Part 2, A deposit to account for. No crime needed to fail. A figure in a warm armchair while people in need stand outside the window" maxWidth={1100} />

That raises a startling distinction. Under this teaching, someone does not have to steal, cheat, or commit a crime to fail the test of wealth. A person may acquire a fortune honestly, live comfortably, and still neglect suffering they had the power to relieve.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-10.webp" alt="Part 2, A deposit to account for. Luxury while others lack the basics. A yacht and jewels beside an empty bread basket" maxWidth={1100} />

The same book goes further. It condemns monopolizing resources for unnecessary luxury when others are deprived of basic necessities. It also distinguishes genuine generosity from giving merely for praise or reputation.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-11.webp" alt="Part 2, A deposit to account for. Giving only after death? A last will, an empty hourglass and coins left on a chair" maxWidth={1100} />

And it asks another uncomfortable question: What is the value of giving away a fortune only after death, when the person can no longer enjoy it anyway?

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-12.webp" alt="Part 2, A deposit to account for. Giving while alive matters more. One figure handing bread to another, a heart above them" maxWidth={1100} />

The spirits acknowledge that leaving money to useful purposes is better than doing nothing. But they place greater moral value on generosity practiced during life, when giving may require someone to sacrifice something they could have kept for themselves.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-13.webp" alt="Statement card: Wealth is an opportunity to do good. Neglecting it matters." maxWidth={1100} />

The principle is simple: wealth provides the opportunity to do a great deal of good. And, according to these teachings, neglecting that opportunity matters.

## Lives that turn around

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-14.webp" alt="Part 3: What are the consequences? A figure walking toward a doorway of light, leaving gold behind" maxWidth={1100} />

But what could the consequences actually be?

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-15.webp" alt="Part 3, Lives that turn around. The spirit lives on and returns. A soul of light moving through a row of arches" maxWidth={1100} />

To understand the next part, it helps to know one central teaching from the spirits. Spiritism teaches that the spirit survives physical death and can return in another earthly life. These successive lives are understood as opportunities for moral growth, including learning from previous mistakes.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-16.webp" alt="Part 3, Lives that turn around. Circumstances are reversed. A turning wheel with a crown on one side and a begging bowl on the other" maxWidth={1100} />

In The Spirits' Book, the spirits describe reversals of circumstance. Those who squandered wealth may later experience poverty. People who treated others with arrogance may experience humiliation. Those who abused authority may find themselves under the authority of people who treat them harshly.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-17.webp" alt="Part 3, Lives that turn around. Not eternal: repair and learning. A broken bowl mended with gold" maxWidth={1100} />

This is presented not as an eternal sentence, but as part of a process of accountability, reparation, and learning.

## Two accounts from Heaven and Hell

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-18.webp" alt="From the book Heaven and Hell: a remarkable account. A glowing open book" maxWidth={1100} />

And in the book Heaven and Hell, a remarkable account illustrates this teaching.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-19.webp" alt="The story of Count Max. A castle on a hill at dusk" maxWidth={1100} />

It concerns a nobleman known as Count Max.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-20.webp" alt="Part 4, Two accounts from Heaven and Hell. Count Max: a lord who mistreated others. A feast table, with laborers carrying heavy loads outside" maxWidth={1100} />

According to the account in the book, Max had once lived as a wealthy lord. He possessed lands and privileges, but mistreated those under his control. His life revolved around power and personal pleasure.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-21.webp" alt="Part 4, Two accounts from Heaven and Hell. Remorse after death. A lone figure surrounded by shadowy faces" maxWidth={1100} />

After death, he described severe remorse and suffering. Eventually, he returned to earthly life under vastly different conditions.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-22.webp" alt="Part 4, Two accounts from Heaven and Hell. Born poor, later disabled. A man with a crutch and a begging bowl beside a hut" maxWidth={1100} />

He was born into poverty. Later, an illness left him disabled. For decades, he survived by begging.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-23.webp" alt="Part 4, Two accounts from Heaven and Hell. Where he begged. A dirt road winding toward the castle" maxWidth={1100} />

And the detail that makes the account so striking is where he begged.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-24.webp" alt="Part 4, Two accounts from Heaven and Hell. On the lands he once owned. An old beggar resting by the castle stable" maxWidth={1100} />

He was said to have wandered through the very lands that once belonged to him, even finding shelter in the stables of the castle where he had once been master.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-25.webp" alt="The owner had become a beggar. A lord on a throne beside the same figure holding a begging bowl" maxWidth={1100} />

The owner had become a beggar. The person who once commanded others now depended on their kindness.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-26.webp" alt="Part 4, Two accounts from Heaven and Hell. A spirit account, not historical proof. An open book labeled “Received through spirits”" maxWidth={1100} />

In this account, the story illustrates how a spirit may undergo circumstances opposite to those it previously abused. It is presented as an account received through spirit communication, not as an independently established historical proof of reincarnation.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-27.webp" alt="Not everyone with wealth must fail. A dawn rising over hills" maxWidth={1100} />

But the books also offer a very different example. They do not teach that everyone with wealth must fail.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-28.webp" alt="Part 4, Two accounts from Heaven and Hell. Countess Paula: restraint and generosity. A woman leaving bread at a poor family's door at night" maxWidth={1100} />

The same book also recounts the story of Countess Paula, a woman born into privilege who practiced restraint and generosity. Rather than spending every available resource on luxury, she managed her household carefully and devoted a substantial portion of her fortune to those in need.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-29.webp" alt="Part 4, Two accounts from Heaven and Hell. Bright and at peace after death. A radiant figure of light" maxWidth={1100} />

The account describes her condition after death in strikingly positive terms. Her wealth, rather than becoming an obstacle, had provided opportunities for charitable action and spiritual progress.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-30.webp" alt="Count Max and Countess Paula side by side: Not the wealth. What we do with it, and why." maxWidth={1100} />

Together, the two accounts make an important distinction. It is not wealth itself that determines a spirit's condition. It is what the person does with wealth, and the motives behind those choices.

## Today's new fortunes

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-31.webp" alt="Part 5, Today's new fortunes. Inequality is made by people. One large gold coin beside a field of tiny figures" maxWidth={1100} />

There is another relevant teaching in The Spirits' Book. The spirits are asked whether inequality of social conditions is a law of nature. Their answer is no: such inequality is described as the work of humanity, not God. Pride and selfishness are identified as major causes.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-32.webp" alt="Part 5, Today's new fortunes. Old teachings, new fortunes. An 1857 book beside a glowing computer chip" maxWidth={1100} />

These are nineteenth-century teachings, yet they pose a question that applies directly to the fortunes created by today's technology companies.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-33.webp" alt="Part 5, Today's new fortunes. A headline can't show the future. A rising IPO chart beside a figure with a question mark" maxWidth={1100} />

Anthropic's IPO has made its founders and other shareholders extraordinarily wealthy on paper. Their future choices—and the good they may do with their resources—are not something a stock-market headline can tell us.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-34.webp" alt="Part 5, Today's new fortunes. Look at responsibility, not size. A magnifying glass moving from coins to a balance scale" maxWidth={1100} />

But the spirits' teachings direct attention away from the size of those fortunes and toward the responsibilities they create.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-35.webp" alt="Part 5, Today's new fortunes. How was the opportunity used? A balance scale with coins on one side and a glowing heart on the other" maxWidth={1100} />

According to Spiritism, the person who possesses wealth is not judged simply by how much they accumulated. The question is how that opportunity was used.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-36.webp" alt="Part 5, Today's new fortunes. Help others, or demand more? A road splitting toward a bright town and a walled fortress" maxWidth={1100} />

Did it become a means to help others? Or merely another reason to demand more?

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-37.webp" alt="Back to the beginning. The gold mansion and private jet, now dimmed" maxWidth={1100} />

And that brings us back to the question at the beginning.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-38.webp" alt="Envy the newest billionaires… or pity them? Figures on gold pedestals above a crowd" maxWidth={1100} />

Should we envy the world's newest billionaires—or pity them?

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires/envy-or-pity-the-newest-billionaires-39.webp" alt="The apparent prize may also be a serious test. A gold medal with a heart at its center" maxWidth={1100} />

The Spirits' Book makes the distinction clear: those who use their wealth selfishly are not to be envied. In its view, the apparent prize may also be a serious test, and the consequences of failing it can extend beyond death.
