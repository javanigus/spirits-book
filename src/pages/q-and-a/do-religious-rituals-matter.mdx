# Do Religious Rituals Matter?

*According to The Spirits' Book*

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-01.webp" alt="Title card: Do Religious Rituals Matter?" maxWidth={1100} />

Praying at set times. Lighting candles. Holy water, pilgrimages, special clothing and sacrifices. Every religion has its rituals, and many people believe God requires them. But do rituals really matter to God? The Spirits' Book gives an answer that may surprise you.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-02.webp" alt="Where these answers come from. The Spirits' Book: published in 1857. Allan Kardec: the French teacher who asked the questions. Advanced spirits: wise, good souls who grew through many lives. Mediums: people through whom spirits communicate." maxWidth={1100} />

First, a quick note on where these answers come from. The Spirits' Book was published in 1857 by Allan Kardec, a French teacher. Kardec asked more than a thousand questions about God, life and death. The answers came from highly advanced spirits. These are souls of people who once lived on earth, and who became wise and good through many lives and many trials. Some people call them angels. They answered through people called mediums. Not all spirits are good, so when we say 'the spirits,' we mean these advanced spirits.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-03.webp" alt="Statement card: Rituals have no spiritual power of their own." maxWidth={1100} />

According to the spirits, religious rituals and ceremonies have no spiritual power of their own, and they earn no merit in God's eyes. They can help people in some ways. But they become useless, and even harmful, when people mistake them for true worship, or use them in place of good behavior.

## True worship is in the heart and in action

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-04.webp" alt="True worship is in the heart and in action. Worship is lifting our thoughts toward God. Illustration: a person with small lights rising toward one bright light above." maxWidth={1100} />

The first point is about true worship. The spirits describe worship as lifting our thoughts toward God. This brings the soul closer to its Creator. True worship belongs to the heart and the conscience.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-05.webp" alt="True worship is in the heart and in action. God prefers doing good over ceremonies. Illustration: a balance scale with ceremonies on the lighter side and a glowing heart, doing good, on the heavier side." maxWidth={1100} />

God prefers people who worship sincerely, from the heart, by doing good and avoiding evil. God does not prefer people who think they honor God with outward ceremonies that do not make them any better than their neighbors.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-06.webp" alt="True worship is in the heart and in action. Charity is the best way to honor God. Illustration: one person helping another, with a glowing heart between them." maxWidth={1100} />

In fact, the spirits said the best way to honor God is active charity: helping the poor and people who are suffering. That is worth far more than any ritual.

## When rituals are useful

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-07.webp" alt="When rituals are useful. When they are sincere and set a good example. Illustration: one glowing person in front, with other people looking toward them." maxWidth={1100} />

So are rituals ever useful? Yes. The spirits said outward forms of worship are useful only when they are sincere, and not done just for appearance. Then they can set a good example for others.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-08.webp" alt="When rituals are useful. When they help us focus during prayer. Illustration: a person praying calmly inside an arched doorway, surrounded by soft rings of light." maxWidth={1100} />

Sacred places such as mosques, churches, synagogues and temples, along with holy objects and practices, have no power at all over spirits. But they can help people concentrate and focus their thoughts during prayer.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-09.webp" alt="When rituals are useful. When they help faith get started. Illustration: a small plant growing from the ground in warm light." maxWidth={1100} />

And for people who find spiritual ideas hard to understand, a simple ritual can give their faith a place to start, and help direct their good intentions.

## When rituals become useless or harmful

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-10.webp" alt="When rituals become useless or harmful. When they are done to show off. Illustration: a golden mask held on a stick." maxWidth={1100} />

But rituals become useless, or even harmful, when people do them out of vanity, to show off, or only because society expects it. Then they set a bad example, because they hide moral faults behind the appearance of being religious.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-11.webp" alt="When rituals become useless or harmful. When the outward form matters more than the heart. Illustration: a large, richly decorated golden frame with only a small, faint heart inside." maxWidth={1100} />

The spirits also said that putting outward forms and ceremonies before the feelings of the heart shows a narrow mind. God loves simplicity in all things. What God cares about is how deep our feelings are, not how a ritual is performed.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-12.webp" alt="When rituals become useless or harmful. When money is wasted on displays. Illustration: stacks of gold coins with an arrow pointing to a group of people in need." maxWidth={1100} />

There is also the question of money. Huge amounts are spent on grand ceremonies, decorations and displays. The spirits said that money would do far more good relieving human suffering and helping people in poverty.

## No power in sacred words, charms or sacrifices

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-13.webp" alt="No power in sacred words, charms or sacrifices. Sacred words, symbols and charms have no power. Illustration: a lucky charm, a written formula and a candle, crossed out, above a person with a glowing thought." maxWidth={1100} />

Finally, the spirits said that sacred words, magic symbols, lucky charms and blessed objects have no spiritual power. Believing they do is superstition. Spirits are not moved by objects, or by words repeated like a formula. They respond only to our thoughts, our will and our moral character. Good spirits are drawn to people with good hearts.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-14.webp" alt="No power in sacred words, charms or sacrifices. The intention is everything, not the gift. Illustration: a glowing heart shining above a faded offering bowl." maxWidth={1100} />

The same is true of offerings and sacrifices. Physical offerings and material sacrifices have no value before God. In all things, the intention of the heart is everything, and the physical act or gift itself is nothing.

<SectionImage src="/img/q-and-a/do-religious-rituals-matter/do-religious-rituals-matter-15.webp" alt="A person with small lights rising toward one bright light above, under the words: Rituals matter only when they make us better." maxWidth={1100} />

So, do religious rituals matter? According to the spirits, they matter only as far as they sincerely help a person lift their thoughts toward God, and inspire real kindness and charity toward others. Without a real change inside, rituals are completely empty. These answers come from The Spirits' Book. You can read a simple version of it at lifedeathbeyondbook.com.
